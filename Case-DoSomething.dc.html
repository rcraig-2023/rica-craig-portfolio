The Objective
Redesign the sign-in / sign-up flow so more participants complete "A Renewed Lewk."
NoteDue to NDA restrictions, I am unable to share some of the progress or intricacies of the project beyond the content presented in the final presentation deck I delivered to the Product and Engineering Team. Please contact me at rica.craig@ibm.com if you would like to learn more.

Overview

As the organization looks to enter a new chapter in its 30th year, the Product and Engineering Team seeks to establish a website that will engage more young people across the world to make a meaningful impact in their communities. I was fortunate to be a part of the transformative change at the organization.

I was tasked with independently defining the scope of the project, and I had the opportunity to conduct user research on potential visitors to the organization's site and contribute to creating designs for the program flow for one of the organization's key campaigns. In the program "A Renewed Lewk," as part of the "Yasss, Green!" campaign, users are encouraged to learn about the importance of clothing swaps for the environment and host their own clothing swaps.

How can we redesign the program experience, starting from either the sign-in or sign-up process, so that we can increase the program completion?

Goals

  • A participant is able to get started on "A Renewed Lewk" program.
  • A participant is able to make an account within the program flow.
  • A participant is able to find information about the environmental impact of clothing swaps and how they can host one.
  • A participant is able to make a submission of proof for "A Renewed Lewk" by going back to their previous point in the prototype via a pop-up message.

Main Themes

I gathered these main themes below based on user interviews.

Theme 01

Making an account

  • The account-making process should be simple and quick.
  • It feels normal to answer demographic questions when signing up.
  • Sign up with an email address or via Google SSO aligns with expectations.
Theme 02

Navigation

  • The inclusion of an account sign-up step in the program flow could be unanticipated.
  • The purpose of the pop-up message seems unclear.
Theme 03

Learning about clothing swaps

  • Information about hosting clothing swaps should be comprehensive.
  • The information should be easily digestible.

I also used an affinity diagram to better organize the thoughts of my supervisor and I, find common patterns in our research and discover opportunities to improve the program experience. I noticed and pointed out that most people became stuck during the third task, which was when participants were asked to imagine hosting a clothing swap for a week outside and coming back to the website to submit their proof.

I focused on addressing the following pain points:

  • User's confusion regarding the pop-up message that should have directed them back immediately to the program flow.
  • Users lack clarity as to why they need to provide demographic information when they are making an account on DoSomething.org.
  • User's confusion about the layout of the Act Section on the landing page.

Prototyping, Testing & Iterating

My goal was to create a sign-up and sign-in flow that could integrate seamlessly with completing the "A Renewed Lewk" program. If the person is visiting DoSomething.org for the first time, I wanted them to sign up and continue with the program flow. If the person is not a first-time visitor, I wanted them to be able to sign in and continue with the program flow.

I started with concept testing with our users, which turned into a higher fidelity prototype, and constantly involved our users for feedback through continued testing and interviews.

Design Proposals

I also came up with design proposals based on user feedback for other parts of the program flow:

01

Pop-Up Message

Based on observations during the usability testing sessions, I found that participants often dismissed the pop-up message. The dismissal of the pop-up message was problematic because the users were lost on how to get back to the program flow to report their experience of hosting a clothing swap, which is how they would complete the "A Renewed Lewk" program. The pop-up message is an important feature because it was meant to offer users a shortcut to the program flow.

To address this issue, I presented my proposal of making the pop-up message larger and less dismissible by emphasizing being able to continue the program with the "Continue" button and de-emphasizing the ability to easily exit off the pop-up. This way, the user would have to read through the pop-up message from top to bottom before either clicking "Continue" to proceed with the program or "Close" to make the pop-up disappear and go back to the pre-program page.

02

Act Section

Also, based on observations during the usability testing sessions, it should be more clear how the Act section is organized. I proposed the addition of labels "Campaign" and "Member Submissions" so that users can quickly recognize the layout of this section and focus on understanding how other DoSomething members contributed to each campaign.

03

Creating an Account

In addition, based on feedback from the participants, there were some changes proposed to the current sign-up process. First, make the sign-up its own step, as step 2 out of step 4. Before, these sign-up frames did not have a step attached to them. Adding a step number might reassure a few participants who expressed that they did something wrong when the sign-up opened up or felt like losing their place within the flow.

Also, it might be better not to make the sign-up page pop up like it does currently and just open like other frames within the flow in response to a few participants who did not anticipate the page to come up that way. Now, the user will be able to read through the "Learn More About Impact" frame and get more familiar with the campaign before being encouraged to sign up. If the sign-up within the flow is still unexpected or feels out of place, Sophie and I discussed possibly outlining the steps clearly on the cover page so that the user can anticipate what is coming.

Currently, the Single Sign-On (SSO) options are Google and Facebook. In response to some participants saying that they do not use Facebook and that they frequently encounter Apple as an option for SSO, I proposed a switch from Facebook SSO to Apple SSO.

The Tell Us More frame now includes a privacy statement because 2 participants who did not answer or partially answered the fields on the frame expressed that they were more willing to answer them if they knew where the data was going and felt that those answers would support their goals on the website.

Reflection

In my capacity as a designer on this project, I assumed full ownership of most of the design aspects. This allowed me to gain a comprehensive understanding of the responsibilities associated with the role of a UX designer, particularly when collaborating with a team consisting of a UX designer, developers, product owner, and senior leadership. As an advocate for the user experience in the program flow, my role entailed striking a delicate balance between user preferences and the demands of the business and product, all in the pursuit of crafting an easy, seamless experience for the users. This internship provided a valuable opportunity to enhance my design thinking. My supervisor and mentor consistently pushed me to broaden and deepen my design explorations, urging me to consistently evaluate whether the user experience could be further improved.

I would like to thank DoSomething's Product and Engineering team, especially my supervisor, Sophie, for constant support and mentorship throughout my internship experience. I am also grateful to have interacted and conversed with other members of the team, and I look forward to applying my newly gained knowledge and skills this summer to my future work.

Takeaways

Embrace the Unexpected

During my engagement in user research, I encountered a range of unexpected insights and perspectives from the participants. It was truly eye-opening to explore their unique viewpoints, shedding fresh light on the program flow in ways I had not previously considered. The invaluable feedback from these participants enriched my comprehension of how the program flow could better cater to users like them. It reinforced the significance of incorporating diverse perspectives in the design process, reminding me that surprises often lead to innovative solutions.

Question Continuously and Capture Every Idea

Upon delving into user interviews and transitioning to the design phase, my mind brimmed with a multitude of ideas. I made it a habit to promptly document these thoughts, either through notes or sketches, to prevent them from slipping away. As I ventured into wireframe creation, these ideas proved to be valuable allies. I recognized that settling for the initial iterations of my wireframes and prototypes would be a missed opportunity. Consequently, I continuously challenged myself, constantly pondering, "Is there a way to further enhance the user experience?" Moreover, I revisited my analysis of user research and actively sought input from my team members. This iterative approach allowed me to refine the design multiple times before arriving at the final version.

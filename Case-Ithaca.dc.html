The Objective
Design a way for first-years to find off-campus events that match interests, schedules, and commute.

Overview

When first-year students arrive in Ithaca, they adjust to a new environment and experience a lot for the first time. Many first-year students come outside of Ithaca and do not know how to get around outside the campus. This project aims to research and offer a solution for this problem, specifically targeting first-year students from outside of Ithaca. We aim to tackle the challenges our interviewees experienced in user research by designing a solution that helps them find off-campus events that match their interests efficiently, fit their schedules, and are convenient in terms of commute.

Final Prototype

Understanding the Experiences of First-Year Students

To understand how first-year students are adjusting to living in Ithaca, we started this project by conducting contextual interviews with 4 first-year students who have never lived or visited Ithaca before. We asked them about their background, their opinions on living in Ithaca, how they are currently exploring outside the campus, and what they hope to do in their free time in Ithaca. After each interview, the note-taker created activity notes based on the raw data, session summaries, recordings, and photos from the session.

Data Analysis

We created an affinity diagram to extract insights from our contextual interviews.

We clustered the activity notes using an affinity diagram and interpreted the clusters to draw insights from the user research data.

01Fragmented Information

The methods of Freshmen learning about event information are fragmented. For now, they need to search on multiple platforms to understand the events and specific activities in large events comprehensively.

02Not Enough Time for Off-Campus Events

Freshmen have difficulty leaving time for enjoying events happening outside the campus because they are preoccupied with their academic commitments and clubs.

03Freshmen often take TCAT buses to get around but are confused about how the system works.

Most Cornell freshmen come from outside of Ithaca and must learn how to use the local bus system. Many new students have difficulty getting the necessary information to navigate the TCAT bus system properly.

04Freshmen love spending quality time with friends.

They want to have connections in and out of class — peers they could do schoolwork, eat, and hang out with. Whatever it is they are doing, they love being with others to spend that quality time.

Based on the interview data, we created a persona to serve as the focus of our design.

Defining User Tasks and Storyboarding

We defined four tasks for usability testing based on the persona's goals. We also made a storyboard corresponding to each task to test how these tasks could look in reality.

01Find a unique experience in nature — Persona's Goal: Gain unique experiences in college
02Add a recommended event to their calendar — Persona's Goal: Staying on top of her academics
03Message someone who is interested in similar events — Goal: Make more friends with similar interests

Final Prototype

A user can select as many interests as they want.
This page shows the user event recommendations based on the user's interests, weekly academic schedule, and grade level.
The user can read the most helpful reviews left by past attendees of the events.
The user earns points and badges by writing reviews for the events they attended.
The user can view their profile with features such as their achievements, interested events, interests, and friends.
The user can view their chat history.

Reflection

Over the course of this semester, I learned greatly about the importance of being passionate and committed to designing a solution for our target user base. We became attached to the user problem of struggling to adjust to a new environment because all my team members and I can recall the challenges we faced as first-year students and transfer students. By getting feedback from users throughout the design process, we realized that this problem resonates with current first-year students and that they seek to immerse themselves more outside the campus. We constantly communicated and scheduled consistent meetings to design the prototype. We did multiple drafts leading up to the final prototype so that feedback from our users could be incorporated as much as possible. It was a rewarding experience to work together with each member who made a meaningful impact on the project.

If I had more time to work on this project, I would like to have expanded the events shown on the "For You" page by showing recommended events based on past events the users have attended. I would also like to explore a feature where users can create events or activities and invite people. I think this feature can allow users to feel more at home if they plan an event or activity that resembles what they usually do at home, such as ice skating or museum hopping.

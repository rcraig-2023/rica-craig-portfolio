Less Than Three A Cappella

May 2022 · Cornell client project

A website for Less Than Three A Cappella, a student a cappella group at Cornell University — designed and built by a team of four over the spring semester. Three rounds of card sorting shaped the information architecture; I designed the carousel, member gallery, and mobile hamburger nav, then shipped the front-end with the team through GitHub.

The Objective
Build a site that stands out from other a cappella groups and makes concert & audition info easy to find.

Client goals

My team met with the leaders of the group to understand what they wanted from the new site. Three goals emerged:

  • Update existing information (members, executive board, etc.)
  • Stand out from similar a cappella groups — signature color and clear group ethos.
  • Make concert and gig information easy for audiences, and audition info easy for prospective members.

Audience

The client wanted to reach Cornell students interested in a cappella — either to audition or to attend a show. It's a cohesive audience: all Cornell students who know Cornell's a cappella culture and share an interest in music.

User interviews

We interviewed potential audience members about their interest in a cappella and music. Prompts included:

  • Tell me about yourself (omit anything private).
  • Have you been to a Less Than Three show? What brought you there, or prevented you?
  • Have you been to any concert, music show, or a cappella performance? What brought you?
  • Have you auditioned for a cappella or other music groups? What brought you to the audition?
  • Before going, did you seek information? What were you looking for? How did you find it?
  • How did you first find out about the show — what sources?

User goals

From the interviews, three audience goals framed the site:

  • Have a clearer understanding of the group and what they do.
  • Find more information about upcoming concerts.
  • Find more information about the audition process.

Content planning & organization

Working backward from the audience's goals, we outlined the site's content: introduction of the group, member information, audition information, concert information, contact & social media, and concert videos & pictures.

We ran three rounds of card sorting to make sure the organization was legible to the audience. We chose the final iteration — each user goal earned its own page: a page for concert and gig details, a dedicated audition page, and an "About" home page introducing the group. A separate members page spotlighted individual singers and the executive board.

First and third rounds of card sorting.

Themes

Three themes carried the site's voice and helped the audience settle in:

01 Purple & black

Two colors carried throughout the site — a way to help the audience settle in and learn more about the club.

02 Music

The site is about a group that makes music, for an audience that loves it — the theme belongs to everyone in the room.

03 Excitement

The energy that keeps the group going is the same energy that draws people to the site.

Interactivity ideas

  • Member gallery with short bios — an overview of the group and a way to get introduced to individuals.
  • Carousel on the home page — welcome the visitor with imagery and performance shots without dominating the layout.
  • Accordion for concert FAQ — clickable questions so audiences can find exactly what they want without overwhelming the page.
  • Hamburger menu on mobile — keeps vertical space clean while preserving full nav.
Final design sketch for the home-page carousel.

Layout exploration

We sketched multiple layouts across desktop and mobile before committing to the final directions.

Layout explorations for the About and Concert pages.

Client feedback

  • No complaints on any layout sketches; chosen concert videos were approved.
  • The stylistic choices — purple and black — went over well. More color, more personality than the previous site.
  • The client suggested adding their concert poster to the Concert info page. We mocked it up and they preferred the version with the poster.

Reflection

I learned how to collaborate as a group on GitHub — merging, pushing, pulling, resolving conflicts in the open. I learned how to communicate with teammates to distribute work and hit milestones on time.

I also learned how to build a site that holds the client's needs and the target audience's needs at once. We listened to the client's vision instead of substituting our own preferences — that's the through-line I take from this project into everything I've built since.
